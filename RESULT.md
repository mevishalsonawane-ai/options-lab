Boss, all your books are read in full and in Jarvis's memory: the 13 in the Drive folder, Aziz and Douglas, and all 40

**The 15 trading books, one line each**
1. Wyckoff, Day Trader's Bible (1919): read the tape; volume against price; follow the break the whole market joins.
2. Woods, Price Action: pin bar, inside bar, NR7, three-bar pullback, hikkake; market bias matters more than the pattern.
3. Carli, Divergence: CCI(20) divergence on daily charts, three filters, trendline-break entry, Fibonacci exits.
4. Trader Tom, First Trading Manual: candles, a 4-bar fractal entry, swing structure (no gap strategy in it).
5. Ozenbas et al., Liquidity: how orders, spreads and auctions work; execution, not signals.
6. Advanced Trading Book (epub): candle and chart patterns; skip the first break, wait for the retest.
7. Bennett, Trading Volatility: options are usually dear; skew, term structure, gamma scalping.
8. Sadowski, 9 Strategies: RSI(2), stochastic at levels, MA cross, Heikin-Ashi, Bollinger, NR4/NR7.
9. Heitkoetter, Day Trading: an indicator toolbox, stops and targets, a weekly profit goal.
10. Larry Swing, Swing Trading: a daily pullback scan (Force Index, ADX), half off at +7%, trail under lows.
11. Smith, Short Swing Trading: six daily rules (50-day side, DMI cross, step trailing stop).
12. Advanced Trading Methods (survey paper): what algo / HFT firms do; no trading rules.
13. Porwal, 10 Strategies (India): inside bar, engulfing, 15-min opening break, traps, ADX-EMA, 52-week levels.
14. Aziz, Advanced Day Trading: VWAP setups, ORB, ABCD, bull flag; the 2% / 6% risk rules.
15. Douglas, Trading in the Zone: think in probabilities, pre-define risk, judge a series not a trade.
The 35 maths textbooks (Wilmott, Chan, Björk, Javaheri and others) teach pricing and measurement, not signals.

**5 ideas most worth testing (none run; you choose)**
1. Volume-confirmed range breakout on index futures and MCX (Wyckoff, Aziz): all our breakout tests used price only.
2. Sell premium only when it is rich vs the index's own realised move, with a futures hedge (Bennett, Wilmott, Chan).
3. The three-bar pullback with the book's wider stop (Woods): R87B's sequence held but its tight stop sank it.
4. Carli's daily divergence on NIFTY/BANKNIFTY since 2006 and MCX: R95 tested only intraday CCI.
5. A weekly profit goal then stop (Heitkoetter), and lots sized on the cushion above the Rs 40k floor (Karatzas): risk-lowering only.

**Already rejected by our research:** opening-range breakouts, candle shapes, chart patterns, support/resistance and
Fibonacci levels, previous-day high/low breaks, CCI signals as given, MA / Heikin-Ashi / ADX intraday, fading stop runs
and traps, buying straddles or "cheap" IV, selling defined-risk premium every day (R103), partial profits, trails and locks (the textbooks prove
no exit can add edge when price has no drift).
