Better, and chosen fairly

Each market now runs the strongest strategies from the research. Each one was picked by its record over the ~2.75 years before the test window and then run on the last 3 months unchanged. With $100:

  • Bitcoin: the stacked version made $122.70 and the balanced version $107.88. The old strategy made $93.64.
  • Gold: stacked $102.15, balanced $100.76, old $101.64.

Simply holding still did better in these 3 months ($140.65 for BTC, $106.43 for gold), because both markets rallied. The gain from the new strategies is in risk.

Three years: holding's return with half its falls

Over all 3 years, the stacked BTC set made +226% with a worst fall of −24%. Holding BTC made +222% but fell as much as −53%. Stacked gold made +112% with a worst fall of −12%, against +124% and −27% for holding. A trend follower lags in a straight-up rally like this quarter's, and earns its keep in the falls.

What each account holds
  • Bitcoin:
    • Trend with volatility targeting: long when the 20/60/120-day trends point up, flat otherwise, sized to 40% volatility.
    • Weekly straddle switch: buy when the big-move alarm is high; sell when implied volatility is 5+ points above realised and the curve is not inverted.
    • Funding carry: long spot + short perpetual while funding is positive.
  • Gold:
    • Trend with volatility targeting: 12-month and 50/200-day signals, 12% volatility.
    • Weekly GVZ straddle sale: sold when GVZ is rich and the drop alarm is quiet.
    • The original blow-off fade + buy-the-big-drop rules.
The extra data did not make the network better at direction

BTC got 16 new inputs, including 25-delta risk reversal and butterfly, term structure, option flows and block trades, funding, open interest, long/short ratios, the Coinbase premium and Fear & Greed. Gold got 11, including GVZ, VIX, real yields, breakevens, the dollar, COT and the gold/silver ratio. Next-day direction stayed at coin level: AUC 0.44–0.56 against 0.50 for a coin. Three years of data and a dozen more signals do not predict tomorrow's direction; the evidence only supports forecasting volatility and trend. So the extra data went into filters, such as term-structure inversion, funding crowding and GVZ versus realised volatility, and those were kept only when they had helped before the window.

How sensitive this is
  • Option slippage: at 1 volatility point per trade, stacked BTC made $122.70. At 2 points it made $111.84: the switch still made money, but the pre-window selection moved to the delta-hedged straddle. Expect somewhere between $112 and $123.
  • Funding carry: its Sharpe looks huge because the model ignores the day-to-day wobble between spot and perpetual. Its returns are small either way (+1.2% in the 3 months).
  • Gold straddles: priced from GVZ, the 30-day index, as a stand-in for weekly gold option prices.
What did not work, and was dropped
  • Selling BTC straddles every Friday: −16% in the 3 months, with a −23% week.
  • The "implied above realised" filter on its own: worse, because rich implied volatility often comes just before big moves.
  • Gold's straddle switch: the gold alarm sees drops but not surges.
  • Real-yield, dollar and COT filters on gold trend: they cut returns before the window.
  • Long/short trend on BTC: it won the bear year but lost the rally.
Before real money

$100 cannot trade Deribit options (the minimum is 0.1 BTC) or gold options, so those legs are modelled. The BTC trend and carry legs, and gold through a fractional CFD, can be traded at this size. The next honest step is to paper-trade these exact rules on the next 3 months, which nobody has seen, before risking money.
