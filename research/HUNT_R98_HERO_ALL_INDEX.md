## Verdict for Boss

- **Why a hero candle happens.** An expiry-day option at Rs 1-10 is priced for the move the market expects: the ATM
  straddle, the market's own forecast of the rest of the day.
  - A hero (10×) needs the index to run a median **2.9 straddles** one way, from a strike about 2.2 straddles away.
  - The typical move is far smaller. The index ends a median **0.70 straddles** from where it was (mean 0.94).
  - So a hero is a surprise of 2.5-3× what everyone priced in, and it happens in about **1 case in 10**.
  - When the move does come, the hero is almost automatic:
    - a one-way move of ≥ 3 straddles gives some cheap option 10× in 71-88% of cases;
    - under 1.5 straddles it happens 0.1% of the time.
- **When.** Heroes are common, but spread over many options:
  - On **38% of expiries** (911 expiries, all five indices: NIFTY 38%, BANKNIFTY 35%, FINNIFTY 44%, MIDCPNIFTY 42%,
    SENSEX 36%) some Rs 1-10 option ran 10× by 15:20.
  - There are a median 21 such options a day (quartiles 16-23). On a 10× day a median 3 of them reach 10×
    (quartiles 2-5), usually neighbouring strikes on the same side, so you must pick the side and the strike.
  - The run is late:
    - 48% of the biggest heroes peak between 15:00 and 15:20;
    - their cheapest point is most often 14:30-15:00 (and the 10:00 open);
    - the median run takes 60 minutes;
    - it stays above half its gain for only 21 minutes.
  - **Nothing in the morning says which afternoon it will be.** From options cheap at 13:00, the 10× rate is 7.7% of
    afternoons. VIX, the gap, the morning range, the straddle size, the weekday and the index move it only between 0% and
    19%, and the extreme cells are small samples.
- **Can it be done at 75% accuracy? No.** It was tested three ways, out of sample (walk-forward 2023-26), with real
  charges. Nothing came close:
  - **Picking the option:** gradient boosting and logistic models on 40 point-in-time features rank options only a
    little better than chance (AUC 0.64-0.69). The best precision at any threshold:
    - "doubles": 29%;
    - "reaches 5×": 9%;
    - "reaches 10×": 6%.
  - **Picking the day:** at 13:00, "will this afternoon give a 10×?" is predicted *worse* than a coin toss (AUC
    0.40-0.44).
  - **Engineering a high win rate with exits:** across 60 target × stop exits, the highest share of profitable tickets
    is **56%**, from selling at +10%. On the model's top-ranked 10% of tickets it is **72%**. Both lose money: the 72%
    version wins about +Rs 378 seven times in ten and loses most of the Rs 5,000 the other three, an average
    **−Rs 1,119 a ticket**. Every one of the 60 exits loses money.
- **Where 75%+ accuracy does live: on the zero side.**
  - 97% of these cheap options end the day below what was paid, and 85% end near zero. Whoever *sold* them was
    "right" 97% of the time.
  - But 1% end ≥ 10× (the seller loses 9× the premium), and the worst ended 178×. 14% double at some minute, so they
    squeeze the seller during the day.
  - On 13 Oct a Rs 5,000 sale of the 22700 CE at 13:30 would have been a loss of about Rs 95,000 by 15:20.
  - Selling was not tested here: no margin, no squeeze slippage, no broker limits. It is a mirror that shows where
    accuracy comes from, not a recommendation.
- **What a 75% hero trade would need:** knowing *before 13:00* that the index is about to move about 3 straddles in a
  known direction. Nothing in index prices, VIX, the straddle, OI, IV or volume tells that here. R21, R25, R43, R50,
  R54 and R55 reached the same answer from other angles.
- **If Boss still wants a hero ticket:** treat it as a lottery.
  - Size it as money to lose.
  - The least-bad version is the nearest cheap strike (about Rs 5-10, 1-2 strikes out), not the Rs 1-3 tickets.
- **One exploratory lead, NOT a finding** (section 7):
  - one ticket a day, the model's top pick, held to 15:20, averaged +Rs 714 on 716 tickets;
  - the 5 best days are 213% of that, so without them it is −Rs 808 a ticket;
  - its 95% range is −Rs 926 to +Rs 2,630.
  - If Boss wants, it can be frozen and paper-tested forward under a new pre-registration. It should not go in the app
    on this evidence.

## 2. Anatomy of a hero candle

The biggest hero of each of the 350 days with a 10× (medians; quartiles in `results/report.md`):

| index | entry Rs | multiple | index move | move in straddles | strike away (straddles) | strikes OTM | minutes to peak | minutes ≥ half the gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NIFTY | 2.60 | 18.6× | 0.80% | 2.77 | 1.95 | 2.3 | 88 | 23 |
| BANKNIFTY | 5.65 | 19.7× | 1.17% | 2.97 | 2.33 | 4.0 | 53 | 18 |
| FINNIFTY | 1.90 | 22.6× | 1.05% | 3.11 | 2.23 | 2.5 | 84 | 20 |
| MIDCPNIFTY | 1.65 | 19.0× | 0.83% | 2.96 | 1.96 | 2.8 | 51 | 23 |
| SENSEX | 5.95 | 23.2× | 0.62% | 2.66 | 2.34 | 3.8 | 39 | 17 |
| **all** | **3.10** | **20.2×** | **0.83%** | **2.88** | **2.18** | **2.8** | **60** | **21** |

- **The mechanism is the same on every index:** the move in straddle units is 2.7-3.1 everywhere. Only the point and
  rupee sizes differ.
- **Late in the day:**
  - start (cheapest point): 10:00-10:30 51 days; 14:00-14:30 44; 14:30-15:00 **87**;
  - peak: 14:30-15:00 77; 15:00-15:20 **167** (48%).
- **Short-lived:** the median give-back from the peak to 15:20 is 42%. On 63% of the days the hero was still ≥ 2× its
  start at 15:20, because so many peak at the very end.
- **Often a reversal:** 47% began with the index at the far end of the day's range, the option at its most "dead";
  20% began at the near extreme (a continuation). This start is the cheapest point, chosen with hindsight, which
  favours reversals.

## 3. Why: realised move against the move the straddle priced

At 11:00, 12:00, 13:00, 13:30, 14:00 and 14:30 of every expiry (5,355 cases), the best one-way index move to 15:20 was
measured in ATM straddles at that time:

| best one-way move | share of cases | some cheap option 10× | calls 10× (move up) | puts 10× (move down) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 1 straddle | 33% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.1% |
| 1-1.5 | 31% | 0.2% | 0.5% | 0.2% |
| 1.5-2 | 18% | 2.6% | 4.0% | 1.7% |
| 2-2.5 | 9% | 17% | 23% | 12% |
| 2.5-3 | 4% | 47% | 65% | 32% |
| 3-4 | 4% | 71% | 75% | 64% |
| ≥ 4 | 2% | 88% | 94% | 85% |

- **The straddle is a fair-to-rich forecast.** The index ends a median 0.70 straddles away, and only 37% of the time
  more than one. This is why buying these options loses on average: they are priced for more movement than usually
  comes.
- **A hero is the rare day the forecast is badly wrong**, by 2.5× or more, about 10% of cases. The forecast at 13:00 is
  no better or worse than at 11:00 or 14:30: 16-21% of cases move ≥ 2 straddles at every decision time.
- **Puts need a bigger move than calls** for the same chance: 32% against 65% at 2.5-3 straddles. Downside skew makes
  OTM puts dearer, so a cheap put is further away: put heroes started a median 2.63 straddles out, against 2.42 for
  calls, and needed 2.95 straddles of move, against 2.76.

## 6. The pre-registered 75% test

- **Setup:**
  - 40 point-in-time features (PREREG section 5; a look-ahead guard test on every index);
  - gradient boosting and logistic regression;
  - trained only on earlier years, tested on 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026.
- **To pass:** ≥ 75% precision on ≥ 100 picks over ≥ 30 days, with a positive mean net.

| label | model | AUC | base rate | top 1% precision | best precision at any threshold | one pick per index-day | passes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| doubles (2×) | boosting | 0.69 | 14.0% | 24.2% | 29.5% (363 picks, 47 days) | 32.8% | no |
| doubles (2×) | logistic | 0.68 | 14.0% | 24.4% | 26.3% | 26.7% | no |
| 5× | boosting | 0.66 | 4.6% | 4.7% | 7.4% | 10.2% | no |
| 5× | logistic | 0.65 | 4.6% | 6.7% | 8.7% | 10.5% | no |
| 10× | boosting | 0.66 | 2.2% | 2.2% | 4.1% | 5.0% | no |
| 10× | logistic | 0.64 | 2.2% | 3.4% | 6.0% | 5.3% | no |

- **Day-level, at 13:00 ("will this afternoon give a 10×?"):** base 7.0% of 689 test days. AUC 0.44 (boosting) and
  0.40 (logistic), both worse than chance; top-decile precision 7.2%.
- **Exits on the model's best tickets:** on the top 10% by the 2× model, the best win rate is 71.6% (sell at +10%, no
  stop) at −Rs 1,119 a ticket. The best mean of any exit there is −Rs 251.
- **Result: 0 of the pre-registered checks pass. The answer to "75%" is no.**
  - A 25-30% chance of doubling is the most any model found. It is mostly the distance effect: near strikes double
    more often than far ones.
  - A 5-10% chance of 10× is the most for a true hero, against a 2.2% base rate.

## Follow-ups (Boss, 21 Oct): "what if we remove the stop-loss?" and "a profit lock after every +30%"

Code: `hunt/r98/nostop.py` and `hunt/r98/lock.py`. Tables: `results/nostop.md` and `results/lock.md`. Same 394,956
tickets, Rs 5,000 each. These are post-hoc questions, not pre-registered.

**No stop-loss** (`nostop.md`):

| exit | win | mean net | median | tickets losing ≥ 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no stop, hold to 15:20 | 2.9% | −Rs 3,213 | −Rs 4,985 | 88% |
| no stop, hold to expiry settlement (official close) | 2.3% | −Rs 3,505 | −Rs 5,026 | 97% |
| −50% stop, hold to 15:20 | 1.9% | −Rs 1,745 | −Rs 2,771 | 0% |
| −20% stop, hold to 15:20 | 0.9% | −Rs 889 | −Rs 1,336 | 0% |

- **The stop rarely kills a winner.** A −50% stop stopped out only 1-3.5% of tickets that later reached their target,
  and a −80% stop 0.3-0.5%.
- **Removing it mostly lets losers run to zero.** That nearly doubles the average loss, on every index and in every
  year.
- **Holding to settlement is worse still.** Late spikes fade into the official close, which is averaged over the last
  half hour.
- **The one exception does not hold up.** On the exploratory one-ticket-a-day pick (section 7), no stop gives +Rs 714
  and a −50% stop +Rs 1,718, so the stop helped there too.

**A profit lock every +30%** (`lock.md`):
- **The ladder.** Breakeven after charges at +30%, +30% at +60%, +60% at +90%, and so on. Also a compounding version
  (×1.3 steps).
- **The fills.** As the app's `ProfitLock`: the best price is the minute highs, a rung counts from the next minute,
  and the resting stop fills at its price, or at the open after a gap.
- **The baseline.** The same fill rules without a lock.

| exit | win | mean net | median | without the 5 best days | years > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no lock, no stop | 2.9% | −Rs 3,213 | −Rs 4,985 | −Rs 3,544 | 0 of 6 |
| **+30% lock, no stop** | **18.7%** | **−Rs 2,139** | −Rs 4,650 | −Rs 2,181 | 0 of 6 |
| ×1.3 lock, no stop | 16.0% | −Rs 2,159 | −Rs 4,650 | −Rs 2,216 | 0 of 6 |
| no lock, −20% stop | 0.7% | −Rs 757 | −Rs 1,140 | −Rs 836 | 0 of 6 |
| **+30% lock, −20% stop** (least bad of everything tested) | 10.1% | **−Rs 594** | −Rs 1,119 | −Rs 607 | 0 of 6 |
| +30% lock, −50% stop | 16.2% | −Rs 1,161 | −Rs 2,597 | −Rs 1,192 | 0 of 6 |

- **What the lock does.** It raises the win rate from 3% to 19% and cuts the average loss by a third.
  - 43% of tickets reach +30% at some point. The lock saves the ones that would otherwise have died: 94% of the
    tickets it changes end better, by about Rs 6,000.
  - It still loses in every year and on every index. FINNIFTY is slightly worse with it (−Rs 1,434 → −Rs 1,547).
- **It kills the heroes.**
  - The tickets that ran 10× or more averaged +Rs 43,742 without a lock and +Rs 4,977 with it.
  - On 13 Oct the 22650 CE bought at 13:30 made +Rs 84,357 held, and +Rs 1,378 with the lock: an ordinary pullback
    early in the run hit the lock.
  - The 6% of changed tickets the lock made worse lost about Rs 53,000 each of upside.
- **A lock and a lottery do not mix.** The only profit in hero tickets is the rare 20×+ runner, which the lock sells
  early; the frequent small wins it books do not cover the 57% of tickets that never reach +30%. On the one-ticket-a-day
  pick it turns +Rs 714 into −Rs 636.
- **Highest win rate with any lock:** 19% on all tickets and 33% on the model's daily pick. Nowhere near 75%.

## R98b (Boss, 21 Oct): "enhance it, try to resolve it, and catch all Rs 85,000"

- **Pre-registered** in `hunt/r98/PREREG_B.md` (commit `eced272`) before any number was computed.
- **The test:** 26 new exits and two new entries; design 2021-23, holdout 2024-26 read once.
- **Code and tables:** `hunt/r98/hero2.py`, `results/hero2.md`.

**Verdict: not resolved.**
- 0 of 4 pre-registered tests pass.
- Catching the full Rs 84,357 of 13 Oct is easy, but every rule that catches it loses on the other days.

**What was tested:**
- **Exits:**
  - E1 free roll: half sold at 2× / 3×, the rest held;
  - E2 give-back trail armed only after 2× / 3× / 5×, keeping 50% or 70% of the gain;
  - E3 a trail on the INDEX (sell when the index pulls back 0.5 / 1 straddle);
  - E4 a 30 / 60-minute time stop for tickets still under water;
  - E1 + E2 combined;
  - each with no initial stop or a −50% one.
- **Entries:**
  - U-A every ticket;
  - U-B a fixed daily basket (CE + PE near Rs 5 at 13:30 and 14:30);
  - U-C confirmation: buy only once the index has already moved 1 or 1.5 straddles from 13:00.

**Holdout 2024-26, the exit each entry chose on design:**

**Why no exit can fix it** (all 394,956 tickets, 2021-26):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| hold to 15:20 | +Rs 54,298 | −Rs 4,535 | −Rs 3,213 | 7.7% |
| −20% stop | +Rs 15,442 | −Rs 1,129 | −Rs 757 | 6.8% |

- **Every exit trades hero gains against losses elsewhere, and none reaches break-even:**
  - tighter exits cut the losers but kill the heroes, which often dip before they run;
  - looser exits keep the heroes but let the 98% of losers bleed.
- **Break-even needs 6-8% heroes, against 2.2%.** The best model raised it to about 5% (one pick a day, section 7).
  That is why the one-ticket-a-day lead hovers near zero instead of clearly above it.
- **So the problem is the entry, not the exit.** Getting there means picking heroes about 3× better than chance, which
  nothing tested here does reliably.

**New patterns:**
- **Continuation (corrected by R98c below: this is distance, not momentum).** Once the index has moved 1.5 straddles
  from 13:00, it reaches 2.5 straddles the same way by 15:20 on 30% of days (28% design, 32% holdout), against 6.9%
  unconditionally. After 1 straddle it is 14%.
  - The Rs 5 option bought then does not pay enough to profit from it: the move left is about 1 straddle, so the
    option ends 2-3×, not 10×.
  - A nearer strike (ATM) bought after a 1.5-straddle move is the one untested idea with a measured basis. It needs its
    own pre-registration and a FORWARD paper test, because this holdout has now been seen.
- **No clustering.** After an expiry with a hero (any index, ≤ 3 days earlier) the next hero rate was 2.2% (46 days),
  against 9.0%. That is small and not monotonic in the previous day's move, so it is noise, not a signal.
- **A busy morning against the priced move does not help.** When the 09:15-13:00 range was ≥ 4 straddles, the
  afternoon hero rate was lower (6%, then 0% on 22 days).

## R98c (Boss, 21 Oct): "Enhance it more": trading the continuation with nearer strikes and index exits

- **Pre-registered** in `hunt/r98/PREREG_C.md` (commit `e964fbb`). The holdout is semi-clean: the continuation rate was
  known there, but no P&L of these rules was.
- **Code and tables:** `hunt/r98/conf.py`, `results/conf.md`.
- **The trigger:** the first minute (13:15-14:45) the index is k ∈ {1.5, 2} straddles from its 13:00 level.
- **The rules:** ATM / 1 ITM / 1 OTM / Rs 5 options, with 6 index-based exits (stops 0.5 / 1 straddle back,
  +1-straddle target, a 0.5-straddle trail, or hold).
- **An index-only check:** a futures-like trade with a 0.05% round-trip cost.

**Result: fail.**
- **The design pick** (k 1.5, 1 strike OTM, a 0.5-straddle stop and a +1-straddle target) made +Rs 1,063 a ticket on 75
  design tickets, positive in only 1 of 3 years.
- **On the holdout** it lost: −Rs 613 on 74 tickets, 95% range −1,915 .. +866, −Rs 1,646 without the 5 best days.
- **Across all 48 rules:** 14 positive on design, 13 on the holdout, and 4 on both, which is what chance gives. All 4
  turn negative without their 5 best days.

**Why: the continuation is geometry, not momentum.**
- After a 1.5-straddle move, the index hit +1 more straddle before giving back 0.5 straddle on 34.5% of the decided days
  (152 triggers). A driftless random walk gives exactly 33.3%.
- After 2 straddles it was 38.6%, on only 56 days, which cannot be told apart from 33%.
- The index-only trade has no edge before costs (−0.20 to +0.17 straddles a trade, by exit and half) and every variant
  loses after the 0.05% cost.
- So the R98b "30% go on to 2.5" is only that a move already 1.5 straddles out is closer to 2.5. The option market
  prices that correctly.
- 13 Oct: the 1.5-straddle trigger fired at 14:39. The ATM 22650 CE (Rs 35.35) made +Rs 5,447 on Rs 5,000 (about 2×),
  not the Rs 84k of the Rs 4 ticket at 13:30.

**Where this leaves hero-zero.**
- Seven rounds here (R21, R25, R43, R50, R54, R55, R98 a/b/c), covering entries, exits, models, filters and
  confirmation, found no rule that survives unseen data.
- More mining of the same minute bars will only find patterns that fit the past.
- Questions that would need NEW information, not tested here:
  - **event days** (RBI policy, Budget, election results, US data) as a known-in-advance prior;
  - **real bid/ask and order flow** at the strike (the lake has neither);
  - **a forward paper test** of a fixed rule from today, which is the only clean test left for anything above.

## R98d (Boss, 21 Oct): the event-day study

- **Pre-registered** in `hunt/r98/PREREG_D.md` (commit `15a1ede`).
- **Calendar:** `hunt/r98/events.csv`, 143 events with their sources, built by `make_events.py` from:
  - RBI's MPC schedules and change notices;
  - federalreserve.gov;
  - the BLS CPI archive;
  - the Budget and election dates.
- **Code and tables:** `events.py`, `results/events.md`.
- **The event expiry-index-days:**
  - RBI 18, Budget 3, election 2 (domestic: 23);
  - the session after a Fed decision 61, the session after a US CPI release 52 (US: 112).
- **What it compares:** hero rates, the realised against the priced move, ticket P&L, and one fixed trading rule (M6: a
  CE + PE basket near Rs 5, bought just before the event, or at 09:20 for overnight US events).

**Domestic events add nothing: the market prices them.**

| event | days | hero day (event / normal) | afternoon hero | 11:00 straddle, % of spot | tickets held, per Rs 5,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RBI | 18 | 28% / 39% | 0% / 8% | 0.41 / 0.41 | −Rs 4,592 / −Rs 3,184 |
| Budget | 3 | 67% / 38% | 0% / 8% | **1.29** / 0.41 | −Rs 4,821 |
| Election | 2 | 0% / 39% | 0% / 8% | **2.07** / 0.41 | −Rs 4,967 |

- On Budget and election days the straddle is 3-5× its usual size, so the big move is already paid for.
- The basket bought just before RBI's 10:00 decision lost on all 27 tickets (−Rs 4,857 a ticket held, −Rs 1,162 with a
  −20% stop).

**US events (the Indian session after a Fed decision or a US CPI print): the best lead in R98, but not proven.**

| group | days | hero day | afternoon hero | move at 13:00 ≥ 2.5 straddles | 09:20 basket, held | with −20% stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| after a Fed decision | 61 | 51% / 38% (p 0.04) | 15% / 7% (p 0.04) | 13% / 6% | +Rs 3,849 (95% −2,976 .. +13,144) | +Rs 6,013 (−625 .. +14,379) |
| after US CPI | 52 | 31% / 39% | 13% / 7% (p 0.11) | 10% / 7% | −Rs 771 | +Rs 387 |
| both (US) | 112 | 42% / 38% | 14% / 7% (p 0.01) | 12% / 6% | +Rs 1,666 (−2,451 .. +6,823) | +Rs 3,354 (−322 .. +8,296) |

- **Not significant after correction.** The smallest BH q of the 24 pre-registered tests is 0.29, so no metric
  passes. No M6 rule passes either: every bootstrap interval includes 0, every one is negative without its 3 best
  days, and none is positive in every year (2-3 of 6).
- **A few days carry the profit.** The basket's best tickets:
  - 16 Jun 2022, the morning after the Fed's 0.75% hike: NIFTY 15600 PE and BANKNIFTY 32900 PE, 57×, about
    +Rs 2.7 lakh each;
  - 12 Sep 2024, the morning after US CPI: the famous NIFTY 25250 CE day; the basket's 25200 CE did 35×, +Rs 1.47
    lakh;
  - 15 Sep 2026, after US CPI: 26×.
- **Why it is plausible, not proven.** A US surprise lands overnight, and the Indian market can keep re-pricing it
  through the day. The 09:20 straddle prices the open, not necessarily the rest of the day.
  - But 98 days with a handful of jackpots cannot separate that from luck.
  - These days were not chosen for being heroes; the calendar is fixed by the US releases.

**What would make it real: a forward test.**
- Freeze the rule exactly (the CE + PE nearest Rs 5 at 09:20 on an index expiry that falls on the first Indian session
  after a scheduled Fed decision or a US CPI release, Rs 5,000 a leg, −20% resting stop, out at 15:20).
- Paper-trade it from now, and read it once at a fixed count of days. About 10-20 such expiry days come a year, so it
  takes 2-3 years to learn much. That is the honest price of a lottery-shaped edge.

### Where the search ends, and why

- Across R98-R98g, about 9,000 pre-registered rules covered:
  - entries, exits and models;
  - events, gamma exposure, breadth, cross-index and order flow.
- **Every profitable rule depends on a few jackpot days, and none beats luck after the search.** A hero-zero profit IS
  a few jackpot days, and in this data nothing knows in advance which days they will be.
- More combinations of the same signals would only fit the past. The only clean test left is forward: `FORWARD_TEST.md`
  freezes the 4 near-misses for a paper test on new days.

## Honest notes

- **Prices.** No bid/ask in the lake. The 1-tick spread is a model; real Rs 1-3 strikes are often 1 tick wide, but
  thin SENSEX / MIDCPNIFTY strikes can be wider. Brokerage and STT are the main costs at Rs 5,000. Lot rounding is
  ignored (fractional quantity).
- **Overlapping tickets.** The decision tickets overlap (the same option every 5 minutes), so they are not independent
  trades. The 75% test therefore asked for ≥ 30 different days. The one-ticket-a-day table is the independent view.
- **Coverage.** Only ATM±10 strikes are stored, so far-OTM heroes are slightly undercounted, mainly on SENSEX (R25: 17
  hidden strikes on 5 design days). It does not change any conclusion: far strikes are the worst bucket.
- **Hindsight in the anatomy.** The anatomy's "start" is the cheapest point, chosen with hindsight. Sections 5-7 use
  only fixed decision times.
- **Not tested:** option selling, spreads, and anything on BANKEX (no options in the lake).
