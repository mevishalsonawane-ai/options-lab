# R64: a 30-60-100 profit lock and a -10% stop on Donchian, OOV and Solo 2.0 (before vs after)

16 Oct 2026. Research only: nothing in the app changes. Pre-registered in `research/hunt/r64/PREREG.md` (committed
before any result). Code: `research/hunt/r64/`. Data: from 20 Nov 2024 only (Boss's rule).

Boss, 16 Oct (after a paper day where two Donchian stop-outs of about -Rs 3,000 each wiped out the day's profit): "add
profit lock on 30-60-100 for Donchian and other new strategies, and stoploss should be 10%, and give me the results
before and after."

## The answer in plain English

**Keep the current exits on all three arms.** The -10% stop makes every arm worse. The 30-60-100 lock on its own is
roughly a draw: it adds a little on some periods and takes a little away on others.

- **The -10% stop costs money everywhere.** It is hit about twice as often as the current stops. On Donchian in the
  final period: 469 stop-outs instead of 240. Each stop-out is smaller (about -Rs 1,540 against -Rs 2,030), but there
  are many more of them. Also, 42 trades stopped at -10% would have finished green under today's rules (+Rs 50,251
  between them). Donchian's final net falls from **+Rs 55,439 to +Rs 37,321** (stop alone, A3), and its design net
  from +Rs 8,281 to -Rs 89,296. OOV loses almost all of its final profit (+Rs 48,955 to +Rs 3,527). Solo 2.0 falls from
  +Rs 99,172 to +Rs 72,565.
- **What the -10% stop does buy is smaller bad days.** Donchian's worst final day goes from -Rs 23,012 to
  -Rs 19,211, and its drawdown from Rs 92,452 to Rs 70,222. Solo 2.0's worst day goes from -Rs 6,120 to -Rs 5,389.
  So it is a trade: smaller bad days for a much smaller profit. R64 does not recommend it; the choice is Boss's.
- **The 30-60-100 ladder alone (A4: arm's own stop, no fixed target)** passes every gate on all three arms. It is not
  better than today's exits in all three periods, which is the pre-registered bar:
  - Donchian: -Rs 807 in design, +Rs 3,454 in walk-forward, +Rs 23,338 in the final period. The final-period gain is
    not significant (paired day bootstrap p 0.10).
  - OOV: +Rs 7,915 in design, -Rs 3,222 in the final period.
  - Solo 2.0: +Rs 2,731 in design, -Rs 1,619 in the final period.

  This is the one idea worth watching: on Donchian it lets a few winners run past +30%. The gain is small and could be
  noise, so it stays a note.
- **Holding to 15:15 with the ladder (A2) is a disaster.** It loses on every arm in the final period (together
  -Rs 2,61,722 against +Rs 2,03,566 today). The 30- and 60-minute time exits are what keep these arms alive. A
  short-term signal's edge is gone within the hour.
- **Why the "two -Rs 3,000 stop-outs" day happens anyway.** Donchian is a low-win-rate breakout arm: 44% of trades win
  and the average loss is about Rs 1,200. It lives on many small trades, so a red day from a couple of stops is normal
  for it. Its worst final-period day under today's rules was -Rs 23,012 (all five indices together). A tighter stop
  makes those days a bit smaller but makes them more frequent, so the profit drops.

Together (the three arms, 1 lot each), final period: today **+Rs 2,03,566** (2,208 trades). A1 (-10% + ladder, own
time exit) +Rs 1,22,611. A2 -Rs 2,61,722. A3 (-10% only) +Rs 1,13,413. A4 (ladder, own stop) +Rs 2,22,063. The
worst combined day was -Rs 41,682 today and -Rs 29,486 with the -10% stop (A1 or A3); the ladder alone left it
unchanged.

**16 Oct 2026 itself is not in the data.** The lake ends 13 Oct (14 Oct for NIFTY / BANKNIFTY), so the day Boss
described cannot be replayed here.

## How R64 read Boss's rule (Boss can correct this)

- **Stop -10%**: a resting stop 10% below the option's fill price.
- **The 30-60-100 lock**, on the option premium. Once the option has traded +30% above the fill, the stop moves to
  **breakeven**: the fill plus the round-trip charges. At +60% it locks +30%; at +100% it locks +60%. Locks only rise.
  A level reached in one minute applies from the next minute, as in R51's engine. A gap straight through it sells at
  that minute's open, so a breakeven exit can still be slightly negative after slippage.
- A fixed +30% target would sell before the ladder could ever work. So four AFTER versions were fixed in advance:

| id | stop | lock | fixed target | time exit | square-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BEFORE | arm's own | arm's own | arm's own | arm's own | arm's own |
| A1 | -10% | 30/60/100 | none | arm's own (Donchian 30 min, OOV 60) | arm's own |
| A2 | -10% | 30/60/100 | none | none | 15:15 |
| A3 | -10% | arm's own | arm's own | arm's own | arm's own (the stop change alone) |
| A4 | arm's own | 30/60/100 | none | arm's own | arm's own (the lock alone) |

The ladder replaces any lock an arm already had (OOV's and Solo's "+10% once +40%"). Solo's S1 setup keeps its index
stop and index breakeven exit (signal exits on the underlying) in every version.

## The arms and their current rules (signal source: the underlying, always)

| arm | signal (source) | current exits (BEFORE) |
|---|---|---|
| Donchian (R51 Donchian 20) | a 5-min **index** bar closes beyond the previous 20 bars' high or low; 1-ITM; 09:20-15:10; one open trade a side, at most 2 a side a day; never on the index's expiry day; 5 indices | stop -15%, target +30%, out after 30 minutes, square-off 15:15 |
| OOV (R52 Open outside value) | the **index** opens outside the previous day's 70% value area and the first 5-min bar closes on the same side; 1-ITM at 09:20; one a day per index; 5 indices | stop -30%, +10% locked once +40%, out after 60 minutes, square-off 15:10 |
| Solo 2.0 (R57 A2) | the A2 model's one pick a day among four **index** setups (S1 midday, S2 Donchian, S3 OOV, S4 opening range); 4 NSE indices | each setup's own: S2 as Donchian, S3 as OOV, S4 -25% / +50% / +10% once +40% / 60 min, S1 index stop and breakeven / 14:30 |

Only the exits change. Entries, strikes and days are identical, so BEFORE and AFTER trade the same signals.

## Checks that the numbers are right

- BEFORE reproduces the earlier studies exactly, on the same engine and cleaned data:
  - Donchian: R51 Amendment A design 4,518 trades / +Rs 8,281, walk-forward +Rs 1,69,661, final 1,787 trades /
    +Rs 55,439.
  - Solo 2.0: R57 A2 walk-forward 166 trades / +Rs 55,910 and final 128 trades / +Rs 99,172.
  - OOV on R51's clean stores with the app's exits: final 293 trades / +Rs 48,955 (R52 reported 294 / +Rs 48,772 with
    its original exits).
- R64's engine is R51's with one addition, the breakeven step. A test proves it gives R51's results exactly on 60
  random contracts when that step is absent.
- Costs: dated charges (STT 0.15% from 1 Apr 2026), R17 "liq" fills (spread and bps; stops doubled), next-minute
  fills, stops on the minute low with gap fills at the worse open, one held contract, the lot size in force, 1 lot.

## Honest limits

- **The final period (1 Apr - 13 Oct 2026) was already read** by R51, R52 and R57, and the arms were chosen on it. It
  is weaker evidence than a fresh holdout. Only the paper days from 15-16 Oct are truly unseen.
- Solo 2.0's picks are held fixed (A2's picks under today's exits). A second same-side Donchian candidate that would
  only exist after an earlier exit is not regenerated. Solo 2.0's "design" covers only its walk-forward months,
  Jul 2025 - Mar 2026 (166 trades), because the model has no out-of-fold picks before that.
- Donchian's twins keep each trade's side, as R51's do. With the hold-to-15:15 version (A2) this leaks the day's
  direction into the twins: their mean is +Rs 54,949. A2 fails anyway.
- "Big movers" are trades whose option reached +30% above the fill at any minute up to 15:15, including after the
  trade had already closed. "Kept" means the exit was at least +30%. Most big movers were "cut" under every version,
  mostly because the time exit came first.
- One trade (-Rs 11,511, a gap straight through every stop) is the biggest Donchian design loss under every version.
  No stop setting helps with gaps.
- BH runs across the 15 tests (5 versions x 3 arms). Sample sizes are in every table: trades and, in brackets, days
  with a trade.

## Plain-English verdict per arm

- **Donchian: keep today's exits.**
  - The -10% stop (A1, A3) loses money in design and roughly a third of the final profit.
  - The ladder alone (A4) is a near-tie in design (-Rs 807) and better in walk-forward and the final period
    (+Rs 23,338, not significant). It is a candidate to watch, not to switch to.
  - Holding to 15:15 (A2) loses heavily.
- **OOV: keep today's exits.**
  - The -10% stop wipes out most of the profit (final +Rs 3,527 against +Rs 48,955; it fails the gates).
  - The ladder alone is about equal (design +Rs 7,915, final -Rs 3,222).
  - OOV's own -30% stop with "+10% once +40%" is doing its job.
- **Solo 2.0: keep today's exits.**
  - The -10% stop costs about Rs 24,000-29,000 in each period.
  - The ladder alone is about equal (+Rs 2,731 design, -Rs 1,619 final).
  - Solo's winners come from the 60-minute S3 trades, and a tight stop cuts them before they work.
- **The three together:** today's exits made +Rs 2,03,566 in the final period. The ladder alone made +Rs 2,22,063.
  Everything with the -10% stop made less: +Rs 1,13,413 to +Rs 1,22,611.

If Boss still wants smaller bad days, the cheapest option in this table is A1 (-10% + ladder, own time exit):
- Donchian: worst final day -Rs 19,211 instead of -Rs 23,012; drawdown Rs 74,283 instead of Rs 92,452; final net
  +Rs 47,960 instead of +Rs 55,439. But it lost Rs 34,228 in design.
- Solo 2.0: worst day -Rs 5,389 instead of -Rs 6,120.
