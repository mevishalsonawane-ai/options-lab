Diagnosis · BTC and gold · every bar, every horizon

Why the strategies did not make money

The markets move a lot inside every session. This page checks what was on offer, what a neural network can and cannot call at every bar and over multi-bar windows, where each trade gained and lost, and which fixes survive a fair test.

The answerBitcoinGold

The short answer

1 · The moves are real, but most of each session is two-way noise

In the last 3 months BTC ranged $1,999 a day on average. Every one of the 92 sessions moved more than $100, and 74 moved more than $1,000. Gold ranged $84 a day, with 25 of 79 sessions over $100. But the close landed only 41% (BTC) and 46% (gold) of the day's range away from the open. The rest of the range was price going up and coming back. Catching it means calling both the direction and the turn, and a network cannot do that at any horizon.

2 · At every bar and every multi-bar window, direction is a near coin flip

We scanned every hourly bar with the last 24 bars as input, predicting the next 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 24 bars. For BTC the network called direction right 51–52% of the time, but covering costs on the average move needs 70.6% one bar ahead, falling to 53.8% one day ahead. Trading every signal lost money at every horizon. Gold looks the same: 51–52% against 51–57% needed. Gold's slightly better daily hit rate comes from gold rising more often than falling, not from skill (AUC 0.50).

3 · What the network CAN see is how big the next move will be

Size forecasts correlate 0.28–0.40 with the actual move at every horizon. On BTC, the bars it ranks in its top 10% move about twice as much as the rest: 0.59% against 0.26% an hour, and 2.2% against 1.5% a day. On Fridays when an alarm was high, BTC moved 5.4% over the following week on average, against 3.2% after quiet Fridays. A big week (the top fifth) followed 32% of alarm Fridays but only 7% of quiet ones. The study predicts volatility, not direction.

4 · The strategy used a size signal to take direction bets

Breakout long and risk short turned "a big move is coming" into "it will go up" or "it will go down". That makes them regime bets. In the development year (Jul 2025 – Jun 2026, a falling market) they made +67.7% with a Sharpe of 2.0, mostly from the shorts. In the last 3 months BTC rallied 41%, the shorts fought the trend, and the same rules lost 4.9%. On gold the same pattern held: +22.7% over the development year, then a flat 3 months.

5 · The trades were right at some point, then gave it back

9 of the 10 BTC trades were in profit at some moment. Exiting each at its best would have made about +15%, but the fixed 24-hour exits turned that into about −5%. The obvious fix, a trailing stop, made things worse (−11% in the 3 months, and less in the development year): with a daily range twice the open-to-close move, stops get knocked out by the noise before the move arrives.

6 · The fix that works: trade the size signal with options, which pay on either direction

A straddle profits from a big move whichever way it goes, which is exactly what the alarm predicts. The old rule only skipped selling when an alarm was high. The "switch" rule buys the straddle when an alarm is high and sells it when things are calm. We chose it on the development year, where it made +55% (48 weeks traded, worst week −6.7%), against +40% for the old rule and +10% for selling every Friday. Then we checked it on the last 3 months: +11.3%, where the old rule lost 1.4%. Most of that came from buying before the 14 Aug week, when BTC jumped 26%. With $100 that is about +$11 from the option leg alone.

What this means for the next test
  • Use the network for volatility, not direction. Buy straddles when a big move is flagged and sell them when calm.
  • Treat the directional legs as regime bets: size them small, or only take the side of the 200-day trend.
  • For gold, which has no option data here, keep the original rules: they had the best development-year record among the variants that were not tuned on the 3 months.
  • Judge the new version on the next 3 months, which nobody has seen yet.
