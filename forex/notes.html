Where gold stands

Gold rose 124% over the three years and was above its 200-day average on 87% of days. It peaked at 5,597 on 29 Jan 2026. The next day was the worst of the three years (−9.4%), and a 27% slide followed into 16 Jul. It now trades at 4,283: 21% under the high, 6% below its 200-day average, and after a death cross on 26 Jun.

When the big moves come: US data time

Big hours cluster at 12:00–14:59 UTC, when US economic data is released and New York opens: rises run 2.7–3.9× as often as usual and drops 2.4–3.6×. From 03:00 to 11:00 UTC they are rare. The first Friday of the month (the US jobs report) produced big up days 2.7× and big down days 3.3× as often as other days. Monday also had more big days (1.7–2.0×), while Tuesday was the calmest (0.45×). The Sunday reopen shows 2–4× lifts, but that mostly reflects weekend news landing in the first hours.

Where they come from

Big moves came when volatility was already high (2.0–2.2× in the busiest fifth, 0.5–0.7× in the calmest), not out of quiet squeezes. More than 10% below the 20-day high, both directions ran 2–2.5×. At the 20-day high, big rises ran 1.65×. With price below its 200-day average, the violent moves were mostly up (rises 1.5×, drops 0.6×): gold's sharp drops happen in uptrends.

Big drops get bought

After a big down day, gold averaged +3.8% over the next 20 days, against +2.2% normally, and only 25% of those cases kept falling. After a big down hour, the next 24 hours bounced more often than not (only 44% continued). Big moves bunch together: after one big day, another followed within 5 days 59% of the time, against 25% normally.

What the neural network learned: blow-off tops

The big-drop alarm is the strongest result in this study (AUC 0.63). Its top 10% of alarms saw a big drop within 24 hours 9.8% of the time, against 3.5% normally. Its top 2% saw one 24.8% of the time, 7× normal. The pattern is a parabolic run: price about 11% above its 20-day low (7% is normal), stretched above its short averages, strong 60-hour momentum, volatility rising (24-hour vol 1.27× the 20-day level), and mostly Thursday or Friday. January 2026 fits it exactly: +8.6% in five days, then −9.4% on the Friday.

Big rises give no warning on the chart

The big-rise alarm found almost nothing (AUC 0.53, no lift in its top alarms). Gold's big up moves arrive with news (data, central banks, geopolitics) rather than building visibly in the price first. The chart can tell you when gold is overstretched; it cannot tell you when the next surge is coming.

What moves with gold

Gold moves against the dollar (−0.40 over three years, −0.57 lately) and with silver (0.68). Rising US 10-year yields weigh on it (−0.19). Unusually, over the last 90 days it has moved with stocks (0.48) and with bitcoin (0.53), which suggests the recent selling has come from broad risk-off flows rather than anything specific to gold.

Direction is not predictable enough to trade

No network called the next day or the next hour better than simple rules, and trading their signals lost money after costs. Volatility is forecastable: the network explains 17% of next-week volatility, while "same as last week" scores below zero. Use it for sizing, not for picking direction.
