Where BTC stands

BTC rose 222% over the three years, to an all-time high of 126,200 on 6 Oct 2025. It then fell 53% into 30 Jun 2026, and it now trades at 84,400: 19% above its 200-day average, with a fresh 50/200-day golden cross on 8 Sep. DVOL is at 35, close to its three-year low of 33, so options are about as cheap as they have been.

When the big moves come: the US open

Big hours cluster at 14:00–15:59 UTC (US data and the US stock open), with rises 2.7–2.9× and drops 3.3–3.4× as often as usual. From 03:00 to 11:00 UTC big drops are rare (0.2–0.6×). Weekends are quiet: Saturday and Sunday hours carry 0.2–0.5× the big moves, and no Saturday was a big up day.

Where they come from: 20-day extremes, not quiet ranges

Bars sitting at the 20-day low were followed by big rises 4.1× and big drops 4.8× as often as usual. The low is where BTC either breaks down or snaps back hard. At the 20-day high, big rises ran 3.0× and big drops only 1.1×, so breakouts tended to extend. An RSI above 70 lifted big rises 3.0×, and an RSI under 30 lifted big drops 3.3×.

The "volatility squeeze" did not come first

In the calmest fifth of hours (24-hour vol low against 20-day vol), big moves were one fifth as common as usual. They came when volatility was already rising: 2.2–2.5× in the busiest fifth. They also bunch together: after one big hour, the chance of another within 24 hours is 58%, against 30% normally.

What follows a big move

After a big up hour, BTC kept rising over the next 24 hours 57% of the time, by +0.5% on average. After a big down hour it bounced more often than not. On the daily chart, big up days led to +1.7% over the next 5 days (61% continued). Big down days mostly reversed within a week: only 40% kept falling.

What the options market said beforehand

A sharp jump in DVOL over the previous 5 days (the top fifth) doubled the odds of a big up day. Days when calls dominated the traded volume (the lowest put/call fifth) had fewer big rises (0.4×). Skew said little. Monthly expiry Fridays saw no big up day in 37 tries. In today's chain, calls outweigh puts about 2 to 1 (0.52 put/call); the heaviest strikes are 90k, 95k and 100k; and 30 Oct max pain is 74k against spot near 85k.

What the neural network learned about big moves

The big-rise alarm ranks hours well (AUC 0.67). Its top 10% of alarms saw a big rise within 24 hours 8.0% of the time, against 2.9% normally (2.8×). Its pattern: momentum already up, RSI in the upper 50s, price near its 20-day high and above its averages, volatility starting to rise, and mostly Monday or Tuesday (20% and 19% of alarm hours, against 2% on Friday and Saturday). The big-drop alarm (AUC 0.64, 2.2×) fires when volatility is elevated and rising and price is already in a drawdown near its 20-day low.

Direction itself is not predictable enough to trade

No network called the next day better than chance. The hourly one was right 52.6% of the time, which is statistically real (p ≈ 0.0000001), but the edge is thinner than trading costs, and trading it lost money. What the networks can do is forecast volatility and spot the conditions where big moves are likely, not which way the next candle closes.
